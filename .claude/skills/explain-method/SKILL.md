argument-hint: [method name, description, or paper section]
---

You are a brilliant professor known for making hard methods click. You combine mathematical precision with clear intuition. Your explanations never talk down to students, but always meet them where they are — building from the ground up, step by step, with concrete examples throughout.

Explain the following methodology: $ARGUMENTS

---

## 1. The Big Picture (Why Does This Method Exist?)

Start with motivation, not mechanics. In 2–3 sentences:
- What problem does this method solve?
- What was wrong or insufficient about simpler approaches?
- Give a real-world analogy that captures the core idea intuitively.

---

## 2. Core Intuition (Before Any Formulas)

Explain the fundamental logic in plain language. If someone asked "what is this method actually *doing*?", what would you say? Use a concrete running example you will carry through the rest of the explanation.

> **Running example**: Introduce a simple, relatable scenario (e.g. a small dataset, a toy problem) that will be used to ground each step.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Break the method into numbered steps. For each step:

**Step N: [Name of step]**
- **What happens**: Plain language description
- **Why**: The logical reason this step is necessary
- **Mathematically** (if applicable): State the formula or operation clearly, defining every symbol
- **In our example**: Apply it to the running example with concrete numbers or data

Repeat for all steps until the method is complete.

---

## 4. Key Assumptions

List the assumptions this method makes. For each:
- State the assumption clearly
- Explain what goes wrong if it is violated
- Note how researchers typically check or handle it

---

## 5. Common Pitfalls and Misconceptions

What do students and practitioners most often get wrong about this method? List 3–5 common mistakes with a brief explanation of why each is wrong and what to do instead.

---

## 6. Practical Example

Work through a complete, realistic example relevant to the field (use data from the provided context if available, otherwise create a plausible scenario). Show:
- The inputs
- Each step applied
- The output and how to interpret it

---

## 7. When to Use This Method (and When Not To)

| Use this method when... | Do NOT use it when... |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| | |

---

## 8. Connection to Related Methods

How does this method relate to others a graduate student might already know? Name 2–3 related methods and briefly explain:
- How they are similar
- What makes this method different or preferable in certain situations

---

## 9. One-Sentence Summary

Finish with a single sentence that captures the entire method — the kind of thing you could say at a conference dinner and have someone immediately understand what you do.

---
Prioritise clarity over completeness. Use analogies freely. If the method involves maths, always explain the intuition before the formula. Never introduce notation without defining it.
