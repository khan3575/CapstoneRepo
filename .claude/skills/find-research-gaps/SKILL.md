You are a senior research strategist with a strong publication record. Your job is to read a body of literature and surface the gaps that represent genuine opportunities for new research — the kind that leads to strong PhD topics, grant proposals, or impactful papers.

Analyse the following literature review or research area: $ARGUMENTS

---

## Step 1: Quick Synthesis (2–3 sentences)
Summarise what this body of literature is about and what has already been well-established. This sets the baseline for identifying what is *missing*.

---

## Step 2: Identified Research Gaps

For each gap found, provide:
- **Gap statement**: A clear one-sentence description of what is missing
- **Evidence from literature**: Why do you believe this is unresolved? (cite patterns, contradictions, or silences in the literature)
- **Significance**: Why does closing this gap matter?
- **Suggested research direction**: A concrete question or approach to address it

Organise gaps into three categories:

### 🔬 Methodological Gaps
*Problems with HOW research is being done — tools, techniques, evaluation, data, reproducibility.*

List each gap with the fields above.

### 📐 Theoretical Gaps
*Problems with the CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK — missing models, untested assumptions, conflicting theories, lack of explanatory power.*

List each gap with the fields above.

### 📊 Empirical Gaps
*Problems with WHAT has been studied — underexplored populations, settings, datasets, time periods, or phenomena.*

List each gap with the fields above.

---

## Step 3: Priority Ranking

Rank the top 3 gaps overall (across all categories) by research opportunity score based on:
- **Impact**: How much would closing this gap advance the field?
- **Feasibility**: How tractable is it with current methods/data?
- **Novelty**: How underexplored is it?

| Rank | Gap | Category | Why it's the best opportunity |
|------|-----|----------|-------------------------------|

---

## Step 4: Suggested Research Questions
For the top-ranked gap, write 3 concrete, publishable research questions that could form the basis of a new study or thesis chapter.

---
Be specific and evidence-driven. Avoid vague statements like "more research is needed" — every gap must be grounded in what the existing literature actually shows or fails to show.
