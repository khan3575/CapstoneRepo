\begin{center}
    \section*{Abstract}
\end{center}

Getting accurate brain tumor segmentation is essential for diagnosis and treatment planning, but there's a problem: current deep learning methods need serious computational resources that make clinical deployment challenging. This thesis explores a different approach—using graph neural networks for efficient binary brain tumor segmentation on the BraTS 2021 dataset. Instead of processing entire 3D MRI volumes, we convert them into graph representations using superpixels, with each node containing 15 engineered features. This lets us keep competitive accuracy while dramatically cutting computational costs.

We validated the approach through 5-fold cross-validation with patient-level stratified splits. Our 5-layer GraphSAGE model hits 90.39\% $\pm$ 0.69\% Dice coefficient, and when we ensemble the models, performance jumps to 92.92\%. More importantly for practical use, we're seeing 6.9$\times$ faster inference (12.7ms vs. 87.8ms per patient) and 156$\times$ fewer parameters (439K vs. 68M) compared to standard 3D U-Net. GPU memory usage? Just 2.1 GB instead of 8.4 GB.

Through systematic testing, we confirmed that 5 layers work best—adding a 6th layer doesn't help. We also found something interesting: batch size really matters in medical imaging GNN tasks. We ran 15 integrity checks throughout to ensure zero data leakage and full reproducibility with deterministic training.

What this shows is that graph neural networks can deliver a strong accuracy-efficiency trade-off for medical image segmentation. The 92.92\% ensemble Dice score combined with the speed and memory improvements makes this practical for real-time clinical use, especially in resource-constrained environments where deploying massive volumetric models just isn't feasible.

This work focuses on binary tumour segmentation (tumour vs.\ non-tumour); extension to multi-class sub-region delineation (Enhancing Tumour, Tumour Core, Whole Tumour) is identified as a direction for future work.

\textbf{Keywords:} Brain Tumour Segmentation, Graph Neural Networks, Medical Image Analysis, BraTS 2021, Computational Efficiency, GraphSAGE, Deep Learning
